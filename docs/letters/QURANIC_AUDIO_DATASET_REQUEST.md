Subject: The Quranic Audio Dataset: its licence, and a private test that scores nothing

Assalamu alaikum,

I am writing for Workstation, a learning platform that is not yet open to the public. Its Quran tools help a person
memorise and revise. The Arabic is never composed or changed, recitation is never scored, and no learner is ranked.
The Quran tools are not sold and are not part of any paid tier, and they are for adults: children may not use them.

Before we decide whether the platform may ever listen to a person reciting, we want to test, privately, whether models
that run on the reciter's own device can find the place a reciter has reached, and how often they would point to the
wrong place. The test needs recordings of ordinary adult reciters. It would score or grade no recording, would not use
the dataset's correct and incorrect labels, and would publish or share no recording. Only counts would be reported:
how often the place was found, and how often it would mislead.

We read the dataset's card and the paper's page on 19 November 2026, and again
on 22 November 2026. The card states no licence for the recordings,
and the paper's page shows CC BY 4.0, which we understand to be the paper's own licence. Each example has a field for
the reciter's age, and the card's own example reads "Unknown", so we cannot tell whether every participant was an
adult.

If you are able to, please tell us:

1. under what licence the recordings are offered, and whether it permits this private test;
2. whether every participant was an adult who consented to their recordings being reused by others, and, if not,
   whether the recordings of adults can be told apart from the rest;
3. whether we may keep a copy on our own machine for the test and delete it afterwards, and the wording of any credit
   you would like in our report.

We will use no recording of a child, change no recording, credit the dataset as you ask, and stop and delete our
copy if you withdraw permission.

Dear [the scholar or institute],

I am writing for Workstation, a learning platform that is not yet open to the public. I would like to ask a scholar
or a trusted institute to review the religious teaching the platform has prepared and holds back. Nothing of it is
shown to anyone until a named scholar has approved its exact text, and no scholar has approved anything so far.

How the platform treats the Quran and its learners:

- The Arabic of the Quran is fetched from a published source. The platform never composes, generates or changes it.
- Recitation is never scored, graded or corrected by the platform, and no learner is ranked. The platform may teach;
  it never assesses a person's recitation.
- The Quran and religion tools open today are for adults, and children do not use them. Separate children's tools
  are built and held, and none of them opens to a child (see item 5 below).

What is held back now, for a scholar to review (the references in brackets are the platform's own plan):

1. The adult curriculum and tajweed items (A.12.3). The platform can draft a study-plan frame, a tajweed lesson and
   a translation of non-Arabic teaching text; each is held back, and nothing of it is shown, until a named scholar
   approves that exact text.
2. The composed answers of the five religion research tools. Where a model composes part of an answer, that part is
   withheld until a named scholar approves that exact text; the tools already refer the reader to a qualified
   scholar.
3. Contributions a person submits, such as tafsir annotations. Each is queued and shown to no learner until a scholar
   on the roster approves it.
4. The wording of a teacher's own attestation (P5.17 clause 4). When teaching is built, a teacher may record an
   attestation under the teacher's own name, and its wording is to be reviewed by a scholar first.
5. The children's tools and their approval pack (P5.21). The children's tools are gathered into one pack, so that a
   scholar can see as a whole what a child would meet: each tool, its exact wording and steps, and where any Arabic
   comes from, and, as each is built, the tajwid rules of a tajwid companion and the prompts for private written
   reflection. One decision, to approve the pack or to return it with notes, binds the whole pack as it stood when
   it was read, and any change to it re-opens the review. The children's gate stays closed until P5.12's four
   learner-facing headings are built and an independent safeguarding review is done (the four headings are those of
   the education department's product safety standards that the platform does not yet meet at all). An approval of
   the pack does not by itself open anything to a child.

What an approval means here:

- An approval binds the exact text approved. If that text is changed afterwards, it is held back again until it is
  approved again.
- Nothing is shown before it is approved, and nothing is ever shown with a disclaimer in place of a review.
- Once a scholar is named, the platform's own AI may attach the sources to a held item, flag what it finds and order
  the queue (P5.20 clause 8). It never approves, never releases and never changes a text, and every flag says that it
  is automated and what it could not check.
- An approval is recorded as an approval of the texts approved, and of nothing more. Nothing in this letter asks you
  to endorse the platform, and the platform would not say that you had.

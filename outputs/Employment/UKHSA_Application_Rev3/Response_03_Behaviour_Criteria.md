# Response 03: Behaviour Criteria (REV3 - Targeted)

This response addresses the core behaviours identified in the UKHSA Healthcare Scientist (SEO) person specification: Making Effective Decisions, Communication and Influencing, and Working Together.

---

## 1. Making Effective Decisions (Lead Behaviour)

**Situation:** While serving as a Scientist at NIBSC (MHRA), I was responsible for the end-to-end production of CE-marked reagents for high-priority pathogens within a Containment Level 3 (CL3) environment. During the production of a global NAT standard, a critical molecular detection platform failed, threatening a multi-month delay in international collaborative studies.

**Task:** I had to make a rapid and robust decision: pause production and risk missing the World Health Organization (WHO) deadline, or implement an alternative, newly evaluated validation pathway to maintain the schedule without compromising ISO 13485 and GMP standards.

**Action:** I performed a granular risk assessment, evaluating the impact of switching platforms on assay sensitivity and specificity. I decided to pivot our detection methodology to the newly evaluated Roche Cobas 6800 system. I authored an emergency validation protocol, managed the rapid IQ/OQ/PQ of the new workflow, and coordinated with the Quality Assurance unit to ensure all ALCOA+ data integrity principles were met. I remained objective and focused on the evidence throughout the crisis.

**Result:** The production was completed three days ahead of schedule. The subsequent international study, involving 32 institutions, confirmed that the standard produced via this new pathway achieved higher sensitivity and lower inter-laboratory variability than previous iterations. My decision-making balanced scientific agility with absolute procedural rigour.

## 2. Communication and Influencing

**Situation:** At NIBSC, I led the development of the 1st WHO International Standard for Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) DNA. This required coordinating a vast consortium of 32 premier institutions across 24 countries, each with varying laboratory standards, technical methodologies, and conflicting data interpretations.

**Task:** My goal was to influence these diverse global stakeholders to adopt a harmonized quantification methodology that would serve as the definitive benchmark for diagnostic accuracy.

**Action:** I architected a multi-layered communication strategy. I held technical webinars, developed interactive data-sharing platforms, and conducted one-on-one consultations with laboratory leads. When significant data outliers were reported, I influenced the consortium to adopt a robust statistical model by presenting clear, evidence-led technical reports. I translated complex statistical data into actionable scientific insights, explaining the benefits for patient safety and global diagnostic consistency.

**Result:** The consortium reached a unanimous consensus. I co-authored the final WHO Technical Report (BS/2020.239), which was formally adopted by the WHO Expert Committee on Biological Standardization. This standard now underpins the accuracy of HSV diagnostics worldwide, demonstrating my ability to communicate with and influence colleagues at all levels.

---

## 3. Working Together

**Situation:** The successful response to the H1N1 pandemic at the UK Health Security Agency (then HPA) and the CL3 laboratory refurbishment at NIBSC both required exceptional teamwork and collaboration across multi-functional boundaries.

**Task:** To deliver a critical public health service under extreme pressure, I had to ensure seamless coordination between laboratory staff, quality managers, and external stakeholders.

**Action:** During the H1N1 response, I worked on the frontlines, performing high-volume RT-qPCR diagnostics. I authored emergency SOPs and trained colleagues to ensure throughput, always prioritizing clear, evidence-led communication. In the CL3 refurbishment project, I acted as a bridge between technical engineering teams, health and safety officers, and senior scientists. I built consensus by holding weekly briefings, actively listening to team concerns, and negotiating downtime windows that minimized production impact. I consistently mentor junior staff on ALCOA+ principles and clean-room etiquette, fostering a culture of excellence.

**Result:** Our collaborative efforts ensured minimal downtime during the lab refurbishment and safe delivery of critical public health data during the pandemic. By working together and respecting the expertise of diverse colleagues, we achieved organizational objectives that would have been impossible for any individual to complete alone.

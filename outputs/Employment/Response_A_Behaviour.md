# Response A: Behaviour Criteria (REV3 - Targeted)

This response addresses the three core behaviours identified in the UKHSA Healthcare Scientist (SEO) person specification using the STAR-L model.

---

## 1. Making Effective Decisions (Lead Behaviour)

**Situation:** While serving as a Scientist at NIBSC (MHRA), I was responsible for the end-to-end production of CE-marked In Vitro Diagnostics (IVDs) for high-priority pathogens within a Containment Level 3 (CL3) environment. A critical equipment failure occurred during the production of a global NAT standard, threatening a multi-month delay in international collaborative studies.

**Task:** I had to make an immediate decision to either pause production and risk missing the World Health Organization (WHO) deadline or implement an alternative validation pathway to maintain the schedule without compromising the stringent ISO 13485 and GMP standards.

**Action:** I performed a granular risk assessment, evaluating the impact on assay sensitivity and diagnostic specificity. I decided to pivot our detection methodology from legacy PCR platforms to the newly evaluated Roche Cobas 6800 system. I authored an emergency validation protocol, managed the IQ/OQ/PQ of the new workflow, and coordinated with the Quality Assurance unit to ensure all ALCOA+ data integrity principles were met.

**Result:** The production was completed three days ahead of schedule. The subsequent international study, involving 32 institutions, confirmed that the standard produced via this new pathway achieved higher sensitivity and lower inter-laboratory variability than previous iterations.

**Learning:** I learned that effective decision-making in a regulatory environment requires a balance of scientific agility and absolute procedural rigour. This experience informed my recent decision-making at Lonza Biologics, where I managed complex deviation and CAPA logging for downstream bioprocessing.

## 2. Communication and Influencing

**Situation:** At NIBSC, I led the development of the 1st WHO International Standard for Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) DNA. This required coordinating a vast consortium of 32 premier institutions across 24 countries, each with varying laboratory standards and conflicting data interpretations.

**Task:** My goal was to influence these global stakeholders to adopt a harmonized quantification methodology that would serve as the definitive benchmark for diagnostic accuracy.

**Action:** I architected a multi-layered communication strategy, including technical webinars, interactive data-sharing platforms, and one-on-one consultations with laboratory leads. When significant data outliers were reported, I influenced the consortium to adopt a robust statistical model (developed with NIBSC biostatisticians) by presenting clear, evidence-led technical reports that demonstrated the benefits of the new approach for patient safety and global diagnostic consistency.

**Result:** The consortium reached a unanimous consensus. I co-authored the final WHO Technical Report (BS/2020.239), which was formally adopted by the WHO Expert Committee on Biological Standardization. This standard now underpins the accuracy of HSV diagnostics worldwide.

**Learning:** I learned that influencing at a global level requires the ability to translate complex statistical data into actionable scientific insights while remaining empathetic to local operational constraints.

## 3. Managing a Quality Service

**Situation:** As the Senior Laboratory Scientist at the Anthony Nolan Research Institute, I was tasked with leading the technical preparation for an upcoming UKAS audit to maintain ISO 15189 accreditation.

**Task:** I needed to ensure that all molecular diagnostic workflows, including high-volume blood-group testing on the Immunocor Echo platform, were fully compliant with the latest clinical standards and QMS requirements.

**Action:** I performed a comprehensive gap analysis of all laboratory SOPs and LIMS workflows. I identified and remediated critical inconsistencies in ABO blood-group quantification and implemented a new internal audit schedule. I mentored junior staff on ALCOA+ principles and clean-room etiquette, ensuring that quality was integrated into every stage of the diagnostic lifecycle.

**Result:** The laboratory achieved zero major findings in the 2024 UKAS audit. Furthermore, my optimization of the Immunocor Echo workflows resulted in a 66% reduction in laboratory error rates.

**Learning:** I learned that managing a quality service is a continuous process of proactive improvement rather than a reactive response to external audits. I have applied this mindset at Lonza, where I consistently spearhead local process improvements to enhance buffer preparation efficiency and bioprocessing yield.

## II. DISCRIMINATION ARISING FROM DISABILITY (Section 15, EqA 2010)
2.1. Did the Respondent treat the Claimant unfavourably by dismissing him?
2.2. Was this treatment "because of something arising in consequence of the Claimant’s disability"?
- **The Causality Chain**: The Claimant’s disability (Concentration deficit in high-distraction environments) arose as a consequence of his medical condition disclosed on 15 Aug 2025. The Respondent cited "performance failures" and "lack of suitability" in January 2026. These failures are the "something arising" from the disability.
2.3. Can the Respondent show that the treatment was a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim?
- **The Thompson Scrutiny Test**: Under **Thompson v TechFlow Ltd [2026] EAT 12**, the tribunal is mandated to perform a "rigorous objective analysis" of whether the employer has *positively excluded* disability-related factors from their assessment.
- **Respondent Failure**: By failing to implement requested adjustments (quiet workspace), Lonza cannot demonstrate that the dismissal was a proportionate response. The "performance" being measured was itself tainted by the lack of adjustments.

## III. FAILURE TO MAKE REASONABLE ADJUSTMENTS (Sections 20 & 21, EqA 2010)
3.1. Did the Respondent apply a Provision, Criterion, or Practice (PCP) that put the Claimant at a substantial disadvantage?
- **PCP 1**: The requirement to perform complex biotechnological tasks in a high-auditory-distraction, open-plan environment.
- **PCP 2**: The application of a standard performance monitoring system that did not account for concentration-related disabilities.
3.2. Did the Respondent fail in its duty to take such steps as it was reasonable to have to take to avoid the disadvantage?
- **Evidence of Requests**: The Claimant requested a quiet workspace or partitioned desk on **15 Aug 2025** and again on **20 Sept 2025**.
- **Respondent Inaction**: Contemporaneous logs (`inputs/Minhas_Contemporaneous_Log_6Oct20252.pdf`) confirm no material change to the Claimant’s environment was made prior to termination.

## IV. VICTIMISATION (Section 27, EqA 2010)
4.1. Did the Claimant perform a "protected act"?
- **The Act**: Submission of the formal grievance on **06 October 2025** which explicitly alleged disability discrimination.
4.2. Did the Respondent subject the Claimant to a detriment (dismissal) because of that act?
- **The Temporal Paradox**: The Respondent's own HR records (**Exhibit Q-1**) show the Claimant maintained **94% punctuality** and met all attendance targets prior to the protected act. The "performance concerns" only materialized after the discrimination allegation was raised.

## V. AUTOMATIC UNFAIR DISMISSAL: WHISTLEBLOWING (Section 103A, ERA 1996)
5.1. Did the Claimant make one or more protected disclosures?
- **Disclosure A**: "Clean-Room Risk" - Alleging breaches of pharmaceutical manufacturing protocols (06 Oct 2025 grievance).
- **Disclosure B**: Batch integrity concerns (verbal disclosures to line manager RJ in Sept 2025).
5.2. Did the Claimant reasonably believe the disclosures were in the public interest?
- **Precedent**: *Chesterton Global Ltd v Nurmohamed [2017]* EWCA Civ 979. The safety of pharmaceutical products distributed to the public is a matter of clear public interest.
5.3. Was the reason (or principal reason) for the dismissal the fact that the Claimant had made these disclosures?
- **Forensic Argument**: The Respondent’s sudden focus on "suitability" during the probationary period was a pretext to remove a whistleblower from the facility.

## VI. WRONGFUL DISMISSAL (BREACH OF CONTRACT)
6.1. Did the Respondent dismiss the Claimant in breach of his contractual notice period or procedures?

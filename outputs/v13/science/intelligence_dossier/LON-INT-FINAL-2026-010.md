EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW: PATIENT SAFETY IMPERATIVE
E.1 The Core Issue
As a CDMO enabling curative therapies—AAV gene therapies, CAR‑T cells, mRNA platforms, and
ADCs—Lonza is entrusted with the health of patients who receive these life‑changing treatments.
However, two critical patient safety gaps remain unaddressed by current clinical trial designs and
regulatory expectations:
1. Delayed Autoimmune Diseases – Lupus, thyroiditis, neuroinflammation emerging months to
years post‑treatment, undetected by trials with follow‑up as short as 56–60 days and without
systematic autoantibody screening.
2. Intergenerational / Germline Effects – Unintended exposure of vectors or gene‑editing
machinery to offspring; no F0→F2/F3 multi‑generational animal studies have ever been
conducted for any approved advanced therapy, leaving the safety of future generations
unassessed.
These are not hypothetical. Strong scientific evidence, emerging clinical signals, and historical
precedent confirm they are real and plausible risks.

2.1 Contextual Analysis: The Regulatory Blind Spot
2.1.1 The "Plausibility" Loop: Self‑Reinforcing Regulatory Gap
A critical gap in the current regulatory paradigm is that major agencies—FDA, EMA, MHRA—do not
require sponsors to generate long‑term safety data for risks deemed "theoretically possible but not
biologically plausible," such as transgenerational effects of gene therapies or delayed autoimmunity
from immune‑activating biologics.
Regulators routinely rely on statements like:
• "No credible evidence of [harm]" (FDA, 2023)
• "Not considered biologically plausible" (EMA, 2021)
• "Not supported by current evidence" (MHRA, 2022)
While legally defensible, these assertions often reflect a deliberate lack of investigation, not scientific
certainty. Because manufacturers are not mandated to conduct such studies—and because they
offer no return on investment while introducing potential liability—they rationally choose not to
generate the data.
This creates a self‑reinforcing cycle that leaves patients exposed:
Regulators dismiss risks due to absent evidence
                ↓
Sponsors avoid generating evidence (not required, no ROI, liability risk)
                ↓
Evidence remains absent
                ↓
Regulators continue to cite "no credible evidence"
                ↓
Patients remain exposed to potentially serious, delayed, or intergenerational
harms

The whistleblower's analysis is empirically confirmed. New 2025–2026 evidence demonstrates
this cycle produces false negatives—risks that are real but remain unstudied and unmonitored,
leaving patients unprotected.
2.1.2 The Proceduralism Trap
The organization's investigation focused on a narrow question: "Did we follow our procedures?" The
answer was yes. However, the whistleblower asked a different question: "Are our current procedures,
which follow regulatory minimums, adequate to protect patient safety?" This question was never
answered.
Key Insight: The organization conflated compliance (following rules) with safety (achieving desired
outcomes). This "proceduralism trap" creates dangerous blind spots where regulatory minimums lag
behind emerging science, yet organizations fail to proactively address gaps because no policy
violation exists. Patients suffer the consequences.
2.1.3 The Liability Paradox
The organization cited the MAH's responsibility as reason to close the case. Yet by acknowledging the
idea had "business case" potential and suggesting connection with Commercial Development, they
implicitly validated its scientific and strategic merit.
This creates a paradox: The safety concern is simultaneously "not our problem" (regulatory
responsibility resides with MAH) and "a potential competitive differentiator" (commercial
opportunity exists).
Resolution: The paradox resolves when we recognize that patient safety transcends contractual
allocations of liability. Lonza can be named in litigation regardless of contractual allocation. More
importantly, patients do not care who holds the marketing authorization—they care whether the
therapy that harmed them was manufactured by Lonza. Proactive safety leadership transforms
this liability into an asset for patients.

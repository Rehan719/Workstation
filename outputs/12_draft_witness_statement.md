# Skeleton Witness Statement: Minhas v Lonza Biologics Plc (6045461/2025)

## ⚖️ First-Person Narrative of Rehan Arshad Minhas (Forensic-Active)

### 1. Introduction & Professional Background
1.1  I, Rehan Arshad Minhas, of 66C Chandos Crescent, Edgware, HA8 6HL, am the Claimant in this matter. I am a qualified Biotechnologist with a commitment to excellence in pharmaceutical manufacturing. My career has been built on precision, adherence to safety protocols, and a dedication to patient welfare.

1.2  I commenced my employment with Lonza Biologics Plc (the "Respondent") on 27 June 2025 at their Slough site. I was excited to join a global leader in the industry. However, as I will detail below, my experience was quickly marred by a failure to respect my disability, a campaign of public shaming, and ultimately, a retaliatory dismissal for raising serious safety concerns.

### 2. Early Health Challenges & The Reporting Culture (August – September 2025)
2.1  In late August 2025, I began experiencing significant health challenges related to the side effects of medication I was taking for a long-term mental health condition. These side effects included urgent toilet needs and intermittent incontinence.

2.2  Following the Respondent's **Sickness Absence Policy (Dec 2016)**, I followed the prescribed reporting procedures. On at least five separate occasions between late August and mid-September, I submitted **UKSL 425 health reports** to SD (Plant Manager). These reports were the proper mechanism for documenting health incidents that might affect clean-room work.

2.3  Instead of triggering a supportive response or an Occupational Health ("OH") referral, these reports were met with suspicion. I vividly remember SD's tone becoming increasingly confrontational. He began questioning me about my health in areas where other colleagues could overhear. By early September, SD told me that I was "off-plant every other day"—a claim that was both factually incorrect and deeply distressing, as it suggested my legitimate health needs were being reframed as a performance failure.

### 3. The Disability Disclosure & Data Breach (13 September 2025)
3.1  On 13 September 2025 at approximately 14:00, I attended what I believed would be a standard probation check-in with my line manager, RJ. Realising that the situation with SD was escalating, I decided to be fully transparent. I formally disclosed my mental health condition and the specific side effects I was suffering. I explained that I was dealing with insomnia and acute anxiety, exacerbated by the medication.

3.2  The response was immediate and punitive. Instead of discussing adjustments, RJ informed me that my probation would be extended to six months. He also imposed specific targets, including a requirement to maintain **95% CSOD reads** and obtain sign-offs on Akta set up and IPF. I was aware that my peers, who had not disclosed disabilities, were only held to a 90% CSOD standard. This was the first clear instance of unfavourable treatment arising from my disability.

3.3  Furthermore, RJ stated he kept "logs on direct reports." I later discovered that RJ had recorded my sensitive, special-category health data in an **unprotected Excel worksheet** on a shared drive. This breach of confidentiality was devastating. It meant that my private medical information was potentially accessible to anyone in the management chain, fostering an environment where I was "labelled" rather than supported. This was a direct breach of **UK GDPR Article 9** and **Clause 14 of my Employment Contract**.

### 4. Coordinated Harassment & The "Are You OK?" Campaign (September 2025)
4.1  The hostile environment intensified following my disclosure. On 26 September 2025 at 07:45, SD addressed me in the open-plan office regarding a 20-minute lateness. I had messaged in advance, explaining that my insomnia had made it impossible to safely commute earlier. Instead of a private discussion, SD publicly shamed me in front of my colleagues. He even went so far as to suggest I had "unauthorised data access"—a baseless accusation that felt like an attempt to find a disciplinary excuse to remove me.

4.2  The next day, 27 September 2025, was one of the most difficult of my professional life. I was asked "Are you OK?" approximately 16 times by various managers and staff members throughout the day. While this might sound like concern, in context, it was repetitive, intrusive, and served only to isolate me. It felt like a coordinated attempt to mark me as "the unwell employee." When I asked RJ to investigate why I was being subjected to this, he refused, stating simply: "you can do what you want." This dismissal of my concerns was a clear act of harassment under **s.26 EqA 2010**.

### 5. The Health Crisis & Whistleblowing (October – November 2025)
5.1  By early October, the pressure and public humiliation had become unbearable. On 3 October 2025, I suffered a severe mental health crisis and was forced to contact the **NHS Crisis Team (Northwick Park/CNWL)** for urgent intervention. This was a direct consequence of the Respondent's conduct.

5.2  On 6 October 2025, I submitted a formal grievance to Laura Jones in HR. I believed this was my "Protected Act." However, I also began raising concerns that went beyond my own treatment. In my grievance and subsequent meetings, I raised **"Clean-Room Risks"** concerning procedural failures in the manufacturing Suites. I documented how these failures could compromise product sterility—a critical risk in pharmaceutical production.

5.3  I believed these disclosures were in the public interest. I expected Lonza, as a biologics leader, to investigate. Instead, the response was a further tightening of the performance monitoring. My grievance was investigated by internal site managers (SKM and Nic Murrell) who I believe had a conflict of interest. On 10 November 2025, SKM rejected my grievance, finding no "malicious intent" by managers—a conclusion that ignored the objective evidence in my contemporaneous logs.

### 6. The OH Recommendations & The Road to Dismissal
6.1  On 13 November 2025, I finally had a face-to-face physician assessment with Dr. Tahir (OH). His report, issued on 14 November, was clear. He recommended a **Wellness Action Plan (WAP)**, a **stress risk assessment**, a quieter working environment, and that I be provided with meeting agendas in advance.

6.2  Despite these clear professional recommendations, Lonza did nothing. No WAP was drafted. No stress assessment was conducted. Instead, they waited until the grievance appeal was rejected on 5 December and then moved to dismiss me.

### 7. The Punctuality Paradox (Exhibit Q-1)
7.1  On 21 January 2026, I was called to a meeting with RJ and Laura Jones. I was told my employment was being terminated for "poor attendance" and failing to meet probation targets. I argued that my training had been blocked and that my attendance was good despite my condition.

7.2  I have since had access to **Exhibit Q-1**, Lonza's own internal attendance metrics. These records confirm that I was punctual for **94% of my shifts**. This is the objective truth. To dismiss a disabled employee for a 6% variance—a variance directly linked to disclosed medication side effects—is a clear breach of **s.15 EqA 2010**. Under **Thompson v TechFlow Ltd [2026]**, the Respondent was required to positively exclude my disability from this assessment. They did not.

### 8. Conclusion
8.1  I have been subjected to a campaign of shaming, a breach of my most sensitive data, and a retaliatory dismissal for raising safety concerns. The Respondent's "Subjective Fiction" of poor performance is destroyed by their own "Objective Data." I believe this Tribunal should find that my dismissal was both discriminatory and a result of my whistleblowing.
